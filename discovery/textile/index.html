1733–1785 · A PARALLEL REVOLUTION

Faster hands.
More thread.

Two transformations unfolded alongside the pattern problem. One accelerated yarn production; another accelerated weaving. Neither, by itself, told a loom which intricate pattern to make.

1733

Flying shuttle

John Kay's shuttle speeds the weft across wider looms.

c. 1760s

Spinning jenny

Hargreaves' hand-powered machine spins several yarns at once.

1785 patent

Power loom

Cartwright patents mechanized weaving; adoption takes decades.

ALSO IMPORTANT: 1769 Arkwright's water frame used powered rollers to spin yarn. 1779 Crompton's mule combined features of earlier spinning methods. The photos show later surviving machines or examples, not the inventor's first prototypes.
LYON · FRANCE · 1725

What if the
instructions lived
outside the loom?

Basile Bouchon's idea: mark instructions as holes in paper. The paper presses against sensing needles. Where there is a hole, a needle can pass; where there is no hole, it is displaced.

BOUCHON'S PERFORATED PAPER · SIMPLIFIED MODEL
The paper is not a picture of a loom. It is a sequence of instructions for one.
The remarkable change: the weaving pattern begins to exist as an external, reusable description.

Pattern assistant

On a Jacquard-equipped figured loom, punched cards could remove the need for a person repeatedly lifting selected warp groups. This task was directly displaced.

SEPARATE, MUCH LARGER ECONOMIC SHOCK

Power looms and factory production

They reduced demand for independent handloom weaving. Historical research finds weekly handloom-weaver nominal earnings falling from about 240 old pence (1806) to 99 (1820) in Britain. Do not attribute that entire decline to Jacquard cards.
