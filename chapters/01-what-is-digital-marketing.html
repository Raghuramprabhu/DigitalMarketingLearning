What is digital marketing?

Most people define it as "marketing on the internet." That's true, and it misses the point entirely. By the end of this page you'll know what actually changed, and you'll be able to say it in one sentence.

Two bets on ₹2,000

Meena runs a small dosa shop. Business is slow, so she spends ₹2,000 printing 1,000 pamphlets and has a boy hand them out at the bus stop for a week.

Sales go up a little. Maybe. Was it the pamphlets? The rain stopping? The new college term? Which of the 1,000 people came in? She has no idea. She'll never know if the ₹2,000 was well spent.

The next month her nephew offers to try something. He spends the same ₹2,000 on an Instagram ad: two photos (a plain dosa, a crispy ghee roast) shown only to people living within 3 km of the shop, with a "Order on WhatsApp" button.

The next morning he shows her his phone:

  • 8,400 people saw the ad
  • 260 tapped on it
  • 31 placed a WhatsApp order
  • The ghee roast photo got 3× more taps than the plain dosa

Next week, Meena drops the plain-dosa photo and puts all the money behind the ghee roast.

🔎 Pause and notice Same shop. Same money. Same goal. What's the one thing the nephew had that Meena's pamphlets never gave her? Hold your answer. We'll name it below.

Strip it to first principles

Forget the internet for a second. What is marketing, at its root? Every marketer since the first market stall has had exactly three jobs:

🎯

1. Reach

Get in front of the right people.

💬

2. Persuade

Say something that makes them care.

👉

3. Convert

Make it easy to act: buy, call, sign up.

Meena's pamphlets did all three. So did the Instagram ad. So what's actually different?

💡 The answer: a feedback loop Pamphlets are a one-way shout into a crowd. Digital channels talk back. Every view, tap and order leaves a trace you can count. And what you can count, you can improve.

A department-store pioneer once complained, so the story goes, that half his advertising money was wasted but he couldn't tell which half. For about a hundred years, that was simply the cost of doing business. Digital marketing is, more than anything, the answer to his complaint.

The definition

Now we've earned it. Tap each underlined part to see why it's there.

Digital marketing is using internet-connected channels to reach specific people, persuade them to take an action, and measure every step — so you can keep improving what works.
👆 Tap any underlined part.
🧠 Compress it If you remember nothing else: digital marketing = marketing with a feedback loop. Reach → Persuade → Convert, then Measure → Improve, round and round.

The channels: where the pipes run

Each channel is a different road to the same three jobs. Tap a card to open it. You'll get a full chapter on each later.

🗂️ A mental model: Owned · Earned · Paid Owned is what you control (website, email list). Earned is what others give you for free (shares, reviews, word of mouth). Paid is what you rent (ads). Meena's Instagram ad? Paid. A customer posting her ghee roast? Earned.

5 words you'll use every day

These are the counting units of the feedback loop. Map each one back to Meena's numbers.

Impression
One time your ad or post appeared on someone's screen. Meena: 8,400 people saw it.
Click
Someone tapped it. Meena: 260.
CTR (click-through rate)
Clicks ÷ impressions. "Of everyone who saw it, how many cared enough to tap?" 260 ÷ 8,400 ≈ 3.1%.
Conversion
The action you actually wanted: an order, a sign-up, a call. Meena: 31 orders.
Conversion rate
Conversions ÷ clicks. "Of those who tapped, how many did the thing?" 31 ÷ 260 ≈ 11.9%.

Play: Meena's dashboard

Change the numbers and watch the funnel shrink. Try this: the ghee roast photo doubles the clicks to 520, but orders stay at 31. What happens to each rate? What would that tell Meena?

—
CTR: is the ad attractive?
—
Conversion rate: is the offer convincing?
🔍 The insight hiding here CTR and conversion rate diagnose different problems. Low CTR means the ad isn't grabbing people. Fix the photo or headline. High CTR but low conversion means the ad works but something after the tap is losing them: the menu, the price, the WhatsApp flow. Two numbers, two different repairs.

Quiz: pull it out of memory

Don't scroll up. Guessing wrong and then seeing why is one of the fastest ways to learn.

1. What is the single biggest thing digital adds that traditional marketing lacked?
TV already reached huge audiences. What it couldn't do was tell you, step by step, who responded. Measurement is what turns marketing into a loop.
2. An ad gets 10,000 impressions and 200 clicks. What's the CTR?
CTR = clicks ÷ impressions = 200 ÷ 10,000 = 0.02 = 2%.
3. Lots of people tap your ad, but almost nobody orders. Where's the problem most likely?
A high CTR proves the ad is working. The leak is downstream, so the conversion rate is the number pointing at it.
4. A happy customer posts a reel of Meena's ghee roast. That's which kind of media?
Nobody paid for it and Meena doesn't control it. She earned it. Earned media is the most trusted kind, and also the hardest to get.
5. Why did Meena's nephew show the ad only to people within 3 km?
That's targeting: reaching specific people. Fewer impressions, but each one is worth more. Digital rewards precision over volume.

🛠️ Your turn: apply it

Pick a real business you know: a restaurant you love, a shop near home, a friend's side hustle. Answer these four in a notebook:

  1. Reach: Who exactly should see their ads? (Be specific: age, location, interest.)
  2. Persuade: What's the one message or photo that would stop those people scrolling?
  3. Convert: What's the single action you want them to take?
  4. Measure: Which number would tell you it's working?

You just wrote your first campaign brief. Real marketers start in exactly this place.
