ACT I LEVEL 1

The Balance

How do you find a number that nobody will tell you?

🌙 Monday · 9:40 PM · the auto stand

Kavya's office cab has left without her. Her phone is at 3% and UPI won't load. In her wallet: ₹150, cash.

The auto driver points at his meter card: ₹30 to get in, then ₹15 per km. Home is 9 km away.

She doesn't want to ride 7 km and then argue at the gate. So before she gets in, she asks one question: how many kilometres does ₹150 buy? She calls the unknown distance x:

15x + 30 = 150

Hold that thought. To solve it, we need to understand what that little = really promises.

🧱
First principles

The = sign is a promise: "these two piles weigh exactly the same." Do the same thing to both piles and the promise stays true. That is the one rule of algebra. There are only two moves that use it:

⚖️ Balance: if the same thing sits on both sides, remove it from both.
🦴 Restore: if something was taken away, add it back to both sides.

Kavya's problem: remove 30 from both sides → 15x = 120. Split both sides into 15 equal groups → x = 8 km. She's 1 km short. She rides 8, walks the last one, and keeps her dignity at the gate.

Playground · the mystery-box scale

Weigh the unknown

Every x box holds the same secret number. Every 1 tile weighs one. Use Balance and Restore to get a single box alone on a pan. Try cheating once (change only one side) and watch what happens.

Balance scale
At school you were told: "move +30 to the other side and it becomes −30." What's really happening?
🔍 See it step by step
  1. 15x + 30 = 150Start with Kavya's balance.
  2. 15x + 30 − 30 = 150 − 30Subtract 30 from both sides. Nothing moves: both pans simply lose 30.
  3. 15x = 120On the left, +30 − 30 cancels to nothing. On the right, 150 − 30 = 120. The "−30" on the right is the 30 you removed from that side too.
Nothing ever "moves across". "Move it and flip the sign" is just a shortcut for "do the same thing to both sides". When you get confused, go back to the scale.

Tuesday: the sale tag

"20% off!" Kavya paid ₹960 for a kurta. What was the original price?

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. original price = xName the unknown. Call the original price x.
  2. x − 0.2x = 96020% off means she paid x minus 20% of x.
  3. 0.8x = 960x − 0.2x is 0.8x (one whole minus 0.2).
  4. x = 960 ÷ 0.8 = 1,200Divide both sides by 0.8.
₹1,200. The trap: adding 20% to ₹960 gives ₹1,152, which is wrong, because the 20% was taken off the bigger original price.
🍽️

Wednesday: the dinner split

Five friends split the bill equally. Kavya also left a ₹100 tip herself, and paid ₹420 in all. What was the bill?

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. each share = x/5Call the bill x. Each of the 5 friends pays x/5.
  2. x/5 + 100 = 420Kavya paid her share plus the ₹100 tip.
  3. x/5 = 320Balance: remove 100 from both sides.
  4. x = 320 × 5 = 1,600Multiply both sides by 5.
₹1,600. Check: 1,600 ÷ 5 = 320, plus 100 = 420 ✓
🌡️

Thursday: the fever

Her thermometer says 102 °F. The doctor asks in Celsius. (F = 1.8 C + 32)

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. 102 = 1.8C + 32Put F = 102 into the formula.
  2. 70 = 1.8CBalance: remove 32 from both sides.
  3. C = 70 ÷ 1.8 ≈ 38.9Divide both sides by 1.8.
≈ 38.9 °C, a real fever (normal is about 37 °C).
🧠 Recall before you scroll on
What does the "=" sign promise?
That both sides weigh exactly the same. Keep the promise by changing both sides together.
Name algebra's two moves.
Balance: remove the same thing from both sides. Restore: add back what was taken, on both sides.
₹30 + ₹15/km, ₹150 in hand. How far?
15x + 30 = 150 → 15x = 120 → x = 8 km.

ACT II LEVEL 2

The Line

What if you could see an equation, and every point on the picture were an answer?

☀️ Saturday · 8:30 AM · planning the day

Kavya has a long list: the bank, her aunt's place across town, a friend's housewarming, a hardware shop. She'll zig-zag all over the city.

The cab app says ₹50 + ₹18 per km. The rental shop down the road offers a scooter: ₹250 for the day, plus about ₹3 per km in fuel.

Her colleague swears the scooter is always cheaper. Her mother swears cabs are. Kavya suspects they're both right, sometimes. But when?

📏
First principles

Anything that changes at a steady rate draws a straight line when you plot it: y = m·x + c c is what you pay just for showing up (the base fee). m is how much more each extra step costs (₹ per km). Two options = two lines. Where they cross, both cost the same: the break-even. Before it, one wins; after it, the other.

🛵 Saturday · evening

Her zig-zag day comes to 26 km. Cab: ₹518. Scooter: ₹328. She saved ₹190, and now she knows the rule: under ~13 km, cab; over it, scooter. Her colleague and her mother were each right, on different days.

Celsius and Fahrenheit are both lines. Is there a temperature where they show the same number?
🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. F = x and C = x"Same number on both" means F = C. Call that number x.
  2. x = 1.8x + 32Put x in for both F and C in F = 1.8C + 32.
  3. x − 1.8x = 32Balance: remove 1.8x from both sides.
  4. −0.8x = 32x − 1.8x = −0.8x.
  5. x = 32 ÷ (−0.8) = −40Divide both sides by −0.8.
−40°. Check: 1.8 × (−40) + 32 = −72 + 32 = −40 ✓. Two lines, one crossing point: the same idea as the cab and the scooter.
💳

Sunday's decision: a credit card with a ₹500 yearly fee gives 1.5% cashback. A free card gives 0.5%. Let s = what Kavya spends in a year. Break-even: 0.015s − 500 = 0.005s → 0.01s = 500 → s = ₹50,000 Spend more than ₹50,000 a year and the fee card pays for itself. Less, and you're paying for the privilege. Same reasoning settles buy-vs-rent, monthly gym vs pay-per-visit, prepaid vs postpaid.

🧠 Recall before you scroll on
In y = mx + c, what do m and c mean in real life?
m = the rate (₹ per km, the slope). c = the starting amount at x = 0 (the base fee).
What does the crossing point of two lines tell you?
The x where both options are equal: the break-even. Left of it one wins, right of it the other.
Where do Celsius and Fahrenheit agree?
At −40°. Found by setting the two lines equal.

ACT III LEVEL 3

Two Unknowns

Two mysteries. Two clues. Neither clue alone is enough. Together, they crack both.

🫖 Week 2 · Monday · 4:15 PM · the office canteen

The canteen's price board fell during cleaning, and the new cashier doesn't know the rates. Kavya's team wants to settle a month of shared snacks fairly.

She digs in her bag and finds two crumpled receipts. Monday: 2 teas + 3 samosas = ₹130. Tuesday: 3 teas + 1 samosa = ₹90.

"There's no way to know the prices," says a colleague. Kavya smiles. Two clues, two unknowns. That's enough.

✖️
First principles

One receipt alone ("2 teas + 3 samosas = ₹130") has endless answers: cheap tea and pricey samosas, or the reverse. On a graph it's a whole line of possibilities. The second receipt is a second line. The only prices that fit both receipts sit exactly where the lines cross.

To find it without a graph: make one item appear the same number of times on both receipts, then subtract one receipt from the other. That item cancels out (the Balance move from Act I). One unknown left, and you already know how to beat that.

🧠 Recall before you scroll on
One equation, two unknowns. How many answers?
Infinitely many: a whole line of them. You need a second clue.
What does "elimination" actually do?
Scale the clues so one unknown matches, subtract, and it cancels, leaving one unknown.
Two receipts give parallel lines. What does it mean?
The clues contradict each other: no prices fit both. (Same line = the second clue added nothing.)

ACT IV LEVEL 4

The Square

When x meets itself, lines become curves, and curves have a best point.

🧱 Week 3 · Saturday · at her parents' house

Two things are happening at once. A tiler is redoing the small square prayer room and the verandah beside it. And Kavya's dad, newly retired, has bought a roll of fencing for a vegetable patch.

The tiler is puzzled. "I laid exactly 39 m² of tiles. The verandah is 10 m long and as wide as the room. But I forgot to write down the room's size. How big is the room?"

Room: a square of side x, area x². Verandah: 10 × x. Together: x² + 10x = 39 This time the Balance move alone won't work: x and x² can't be cancelled against each other. We need a new trick, and it's one you can do with scissors.

Animation · completing the square

Solve x² + 10x = 39 by rearranging tiles

🌀
First principles

A problem turns quadratic (has an x²) whenever a quantity gets multiplied by something that depends on itself: width × (a length that shrinks as width grows); price × (sales that fall as price rises); speed × speed. Those trade-offs make a hill-shaped curve, a parabola, and the top of the hill is the best possible answer.

Playground · Dad's kitchen garden

40 metres of fencing. One wall for free. Biggest garden?

The compound wall forms one side, so Dad only fences three sides. He wants to make it square "because squares are biggest." Too thin and it's a corridor; too wide and it's a shallow strip. Drag to find the best width, and see if Dad is right.

Why is a negative times a negative a positive?
🔍 See it step by step
  1. 3 × (−500) = −1,500Kavya carries three ₹500 late fees. Each is a debt: −500. Three of them: −1,500.
  2. (−3) × (−500)The office cancels them. "× negative" means taking away: remove three copies of the debt.
  3. (−3) × (−500) = +1,500Taking away ₹1,500 of debt leaves her ₹1,500 better off.
Negative × negative = positive: removing a bad thing is a good thing.
🍱

Mum's tiffin business

At ₹80 she sells 50 tiffins a day. Every ₹1 she adds loses half a customer. Best price?

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. price = 80 + pRaise the price by p rupees.
  2. sales = 50 − 0.5pEvery ₹1 loses half a customer.
  3. R = (80 + p)(50 − 0.5p)Revenue = price × sales: x meets itself, so it's a parabola.
  4. zeros: p = −80 and p = 100Revenue is zero if tiffins are free (p = −80) or if nobody buys (p = 100).
  5. p = (−80 + 100) ÷ 2 = 10A parabola peaks exactly halfway between its zeros.
  6. R = 90 × 45 = ₹4,050Price ₹90, sales 45. (Today: 80 × 50 = ₹4,000.)
₹90. More money, and five fewer tiffins to cook.
🚗

Braking distance

Kavya's brother doubles his speed from 40 to 80 km/h. How many times longer to stop?

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. d grows like v²Braking distance grows with the square of speed (the energy to get rid of is ½mv²).
  2. (2v)² = 4v²Double the speed: (2v)² = 4 × v².
  3. d → 4 × dSo the braking distance becomes four times as long.
4×. If a car needs 10 m to brake at 40 km/h, it needs about 40 m at 80. That's x² on the road.
🧮

The formula, finally

Do the tile trick once, for every equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0.

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. ax² + bx + c = 0Start with the general equation.
  2. x² + (b/a)x = −c/aDivide both sides by a; remove c from both sides.
  3. x² + (b/a)x + (b/2a)² = (b/2a)² − c/aRestore: add the missing corner (half of b/a, squared) to both sides.
  4. (x + b/2a)² = (b² − 4ac) / 4a²The left side is now a perfect square. Tidy the right side.
  5. x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2aSquare-root both sides (± because a square has two roots) and subtract b/2a.
The quadratic formula is just the tile trick done once, in general.
🧠 Recall before you scroll on
What does "completing the square" literally mean?
Adding the missing corner piece, (b/2)², so the L-shape becomes a perfect square whose side you can read off.
When does a real problem become quadratic?
When the unknown multiplies something that depends on itself: area, price × sales, speed².
40 m fence, one wall free: best width and area?
Width 10 m, length 20 m, area 200 m². Twice as long as wide, because the wall is free.

ACT V LEVEL 5

Growth

The force that quietly decides your savings, your loans, and every "viral" thing on the internet.

♟️ Week 4 · Sunday · Grandpa's chess afternoon

Between games, Grandpa tells an old Indian legend. The man who invented chess showed it to the king. Delighted, the king offered any reward. The inventor asked only for rice: one grain on the first square, two on the second, four on the third, doubling on every square to the 64th.

The king laughed and ordered a small bag of rice. "Fill the board," says Grandpa, sliding it across, "and tell me why the king's treasurers stopped laughing."

📈
First principles

Linear growth adds the same amount each step (Act II: ₹18 more per km). Exponential growth multiplies by the same factor each step, so the bigger it gets, the faster it grows. Interest on a balance, a rumour, a virus, compounding returns: anything that grows in proportion to what it already is.

amount = start × (1 + r)n

And when the unknown is up in the exponent ("how many years until it doubles?"), you need the reverse tool: a logarithm. A logarithm answers one question: "how many times do I multiply?" 2 multiplied by itself how many times makes 1,024? Ten. So log₂(1,024) = 10.

⏳

The rule of 72

Why does "72 ÷ rate" tell you how many years money takes to double?

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. (1 + r)n = 2Doubling means start × (1 + r)ⁿ = 2 × start.
  2. n = log 2 ÷ log(1 + r)The unknown is in the exponent, so take logarithms.
  3. n ≈ 0.69 ÷ rFor small rates, log(1 + r) ≈ r, and log 2 ≈ 0.693 (natural logs).
  4. n ≈ 69 ÷ (rate in %) → use 7272 is easier: it divides by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, and it is more accurate for typical rates.
Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate%. FD at 7% → ~10 years. At 12% → ~6 years.
🛒

Inflation, the silent thief

At 6% inflation, in how many years does ₹1 lakh buy only half of what it buys today?

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. (1.06)n = 2Prices grow 6% a year. Your money buys half as much once prices double.
  2. n ≈ 72 ÷ 6 = 12Rule of 72.
  3. exact: log 2 ÷ log 1.06 ≈ 11.9The exact answer agrees.
~12 years. Money sitting idle isn't safe; it's shrinking on a schedule.
💳

Credit-card debt

An unpaid card balance grows ~3% a month. In about how many years does it double?

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. (1.03)12 ≈ 1.4263% a month compounds 12 times a year: debt × 1.426 every year, i.e. 42.6% a year.
  2. 72 ÷ 42.6 ≈ 1.7 yearsRule of 72 estimate (it runs a little low at high rates).
  3. exact: log 2 ÷ log 1.03 ≈ 23.4 monthsThe exact answer: about 1.95 years.
Under 2 years. Exponential growth works against you just as hard as it works for you.

The summit · derive the EMI formula

Every bank uses one equation. You can build it in four steps.

Borrow P at monthly rate r for n months. Find the fixed monthly payment E that brings the balance to exactly zero. Tap each step.

🏦

Build the EMI formula

Borrow P at monthly rate r for n months. Find the EMI E that brings the balance to exactly zero.

🔍 Solve it step by step
  1. Bnext = B × (1 + r) − EEach month: the balance grows by interest, then the EMI is subtracted.
  2. P(1+r)n = E [ (1+r)n−1 + … + (1+r) + 1 ]Unroll n months. For the loan to hit zero, the loan grown for n months must equal every EMI grown to the end.
  3. S = 1 + q + … + qn−1 and qS = q + … + qnThe cancel-the-middle trick. Call the bracket S, with q = 1 + r. Multiply it by q.
  4. qS − S = qn − 1 → S = (qn − 1) / rSubtract: every middle term cancels (the Balance move again!).
  5. E = P · r · (1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1)Solve for E: divide both sides by S.
  6. P = 30,00,000 · r = 0.0075 · n = 240 → E ≈ ₹26,992Kavya's numbers.
E = P·r·(1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ − 1): the formula behind every home, car and personal loan. ₹26,992: the bank was honest.

Why do early EMIs barely dent the loan, and why does prepaying early help so much?
🔍 See it step by step
  1. interest = balance × rInterest is charged on the remaining balance.
  2. 30,00,000 × 0.0075 = ₹22,500Month 1 on Kavya's loan: ₹22,500 of interest.
  3. 26,992 − 22,500 = ₹4,492So only ₹4,492 of the first EMI actually reduces the loan.
  4. small cut → balance stays big → interest stays bigThe balance falls slowly, so interest stays high for years. That's the red in the chart.
  5. prepay ₹1 now → no interest on it, every remaining monthA rupee prepaid early stops earning interest for the bank in every remaining month. Compounding now works for you.

Linear vs exponential, in one line each?
Linear adds the same amount each step. Exponential multiplies by the same factor each step.
What question does a logarithm answer?
"How many times do I multiply?" e.g. how many doublings, how many years.
The rule of 72?
Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ yearly % rate. 6% → 12 years.
