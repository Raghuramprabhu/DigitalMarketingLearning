Piano rows: two rows, shaped like a piano. The letters are the white keys, the row above holds the black keys.

Full 61 keys (the OnlinePianist / VirtualPiano style used by letter song-sheets): every white key runs left to right across your keyboard, top row first. Hold Shift with a key to play the black key just above it.

UNDER THE HOOD

Three questions this page answers

No sound files and no video server. Everything you hear and record is built in your browser, from scratch, while you play.

🔢

Why twelve keys, then repeat?

A note is just air vibrating. Double the vibrations per second and your ear hears the same note, higher: that's an octave. Split that doubling into 12 equal steps and each step multiplies the frequency by 21/12 ≈ 1.0595.

So key number n vibrates at 440 × 1.0595(n−69) Hz. Play any key and watch the top-left corner: the page does this sum live.

🌊

How do you fake a piano?

A real string doesn't vibrate at one frequency. It vibrates at the note and at 2×, 3×, 4×… (the overtones), each quieter. That recipe of overtones is why a piano and an organ playing the same note sound different.

The piano here stacks ten overtones, adds a tiny noise "thump" for the hammer, then lets the bright high overtones fade faster than the low ones, like a real string.

🎬

How does it record without screen-sharing?

The keyboard isn't made of HTML buttons. It's drawn 60 times a second onto a single canvas, like frames of a film. The browser can hand you that canvas as a live video stream.

The sound engine also pipes its output into an audio stream. A MediaRecorder stitches the two together into one file. No permission pop-up, works on phones, and nothing leaves your device.

🎯 Try this

Hold C, E and G together (keys Z C B). The stage says C major. Now move only the middle finger down one key, to E♭ (D). One key changes, and the mood flips from bright to sad: C minor. Can you find which single key turns it into C augmented, the "dream sequence" sound?
