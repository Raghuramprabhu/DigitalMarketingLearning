Gears that carryPascal · 1642

🚗

You already know this machine: it's a car's odometer. When 0099 rolls to 0100, the units wheel pushes the next wheel along.

Shows 002. Tap "Turn + 3".

Try: Set 099, then + 1. Watch the orange levers flip one after another. That chain reaction is the carry, the hardest part of adding.

3

Adding with light switchesStibitz & Shannon · 1937

💡

Stop thinking "numbers". Think switch ON = 1, OFF = 0. Then adding becomes a question of how you wire switches to a bulb.

a · The relay: a switch pressed by a magnet

Left circuit powers a magnet. The magnet pulls the iron arm down, which closes the right circuit. Electricity flipping a switch means switches can control other switches. That's the whole trick of computers.

b · AND: two switches in a row

Tap switches A and B. The bulb lights only if both are on. Like a door with two locks.

c · XOR: the staircase light

Your staircase light has a switch at the bottom and one at the top. Flip either one and the light changes. The secret is the two crossed wires. It lights when the switches disagree.

d · Put them together: a machine that adds
+

XOR gives the answer digit, AND gives the carry. Try 1 + 1: the answer bulb goes off and the carry bulb comes on, giving "10", which is binary for 2.

The carry raceevery CPU, then and now

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Four people in a row, each adding one column. In a ripple adder, each person must wait for the carry from their right-hand neighbour, like passing a bucket down a line.

🔮 Lookahead: works out every carry at once
Pick numbers, then race.
Ripple: – stepsLookahead: – steps

Try 15 + 1 with ripple: the carry has to walk through every column, just like Pascal's 099 + 1. Then press Lookahead: a helper peeks at all the columns first and hands out every carry at once. Real chips use lookahead; with 64 columns, waiting would be far too slow.
